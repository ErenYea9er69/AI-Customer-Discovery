# Disclosure and the FTC Endorsement Guides

This file summarizes public guidance. It is not legal advice. Ask a lawyer about your case.

## What the guides say

The FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255, revised in 2023) cover endorsements and reviews in advertising, including social media.

- A material connection between an endorser and a brand must be disclosed when the audience does not expect it. Employment, ownership and payment all count.
- A disclosure must be clear and conspicuous: hard to miss and easy to understand for ordinary readers, placed in the post itself.
- Vague tags are weak. The FTC's FAQs say a bare hashtag such as #endorsement does not work. Disclosures buried in a comment thread or a profile do not meet the standard.
- An advertiser can be responsible for what its staff and partners post. The guides expect the advertiser to give guidance, monitor and act on problems.
- Fake or hidden-connection reviews and testimonials are deceptive. A 2024 FTC rule also targets fake reviews and testimonials, including reviews from people with an undisclosed connection to the business.

## What this means for the app

| Situation | Rule in the app |
| --- | --- |
| Owner mentions the product | The reply holds a natural first-person affiliation sentence |
| Owner posts a value-first reply | The reply never names the product, so no connection exists to disclose in that reply |
| A reader asks if the author works there | The next reply answers yes in one sentence |
| Posting under another name or persona | Not allowed |
| A "customer" voice | Not allowed |
| A disclosure in a hashtag or only in the profile | Not accepted by the linter |

## How the linter checks

A product-aware reply must:

1. mention the product,
2. hold a first-person statement of connection (for example "I built", "I work on", "I'm the founder of", "my team makes", "I run", "I maintain"),
3. not match a pattern from the never-use list (a hashtag, a legal footer, "not sponsored").

The stiff form "Disclosure: I am affiliated with X." passes as a fallback. Natural phrases from disclosure-patterns.md read better and are preferred.
