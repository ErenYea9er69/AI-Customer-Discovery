# ICP framework

An ideal customer profile answers four questions. Write one sentence for each, then join them.

1. Who: the role, with the words the person uses about themselves. "Solo designer", "ops lead at a 20-person agency", "indie developer with paying users".
2. Where: company size, stage and tools they already use. "Under 10 people, no finance team, invoices from Stripe and a spreadsheet".
3. Situation: what is true right now. "Clients pay 30 to 60 days late and the person chases by email".
4. Trigger: the event that starts the search. "A client skipped a payment for the second time".

Join them: "A solo designer with 3 to 10 clients, who invoices by hand and just had a second late payment."

## Buyer, user and chooser

Name the person who feels the pain, the person who pays and the person who decides. In small teams these are one person. In larger teams they differ, and the person posting in a community is usually the one who feels the pain. Score a thread higher when the author feels the pain and also decides.

## Stage of awareness

| Stage | What the person says | What to do with it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem aware | "How do I stop chasing invoices?" | Help first. Product mention only at level 0 or 1. |
| Solution aware | "Any tool for invoice reminders?" | Best leads. Level 2 fits. |
| Product aware | "Tidewatch vs X?" | Compare honestly. Level 2 or 3. |
| Switching | "X got expensive, what else is out there?" | Strong leads. Ask what they need first. |
| Unaware | Complains without naming the cause | Customer insight. Reply with level 0 only. |

## Fit tests

A person fits the ICP when all three hold.
- The problem in their post matches one of problems_solved in meaning, not only in words.
- Their size and role fall inside the ICP sentence or one step away.
- They do not match any not_for entry.

A person is a near fit when two hold. Score near fits lower on icp_match and say why.

## Writing the not_for list

Good not_for entries name a reason: "enterprise teams that need SSO and procurement review", "people who want payment processing (the product only reminds)", "students writing a class project". Each entry stops a class of false leads before the model scores it.
