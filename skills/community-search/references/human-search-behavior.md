# Human search behavior

This file describes how people ask for help and how experienced members read a community. Copy these habits when you search and when you triage.

## How people write a post when they have a problem

| Pattern | Example title | What it signals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open question | "How do you handle late client payments?" | Problem aware. Wants methods. |
| Tool request | "Is there a tool that sends payment reminders automatically?" | Solution aware. Best lead type. |
| Recommendation | "What do you use for tracking unpaid invoices?" | Solution aware. Wants names. |
| Switching | "FreshBooks got too expensive, what are people using now?" | Ready to buy. Ask for needs. |
| Rant | "Rant: chasing invoices is eating my Fridays" | Pain is high, intent is low. Level 0 reply. |
| Identity first | "Freelancer here, 6 clients, always behind on admin" | Gives role and size. High ICP value. |
| Validation | "Am I the only one who dreads sending reminders?" | Wants reassurance. Help first. |
| Script request | "What do you say in a third payment reminder?" | Wants words, not a tool. Give words. |

Posts with a number or a date ("45 days late", "since March", "12 clients") hold real situations. Posts without any detail are weak.

## What people do before they post

They search the community first. They post when the old answers do not fit, so a new thread often adds a constraint: "free only", "self-hosted", "for a team of 3", "works with Stripe". The constraint is the best part of the post. A good reply meets the constraint.

## How people read replies

- They read the first 3 to 5 comments, then skim.
- They trust comments with a concrete detail and distrust comments with a link and a slogan.
- They notice a username that only promotes. They click profiles.
- They upvote the answer that solved their case, not the longest one.

## Signals inside the comments

| Signal | Typical text | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shared pain | "same here", "this", "ugh yes" | Raises thread demand. |
| Interest | "following", "RemindMe", "also looking" | Raises thread demand. |
| Tool request in comments | "what do you use for this?" | Candidate reply target. |
| Link request | "link?", "name?" | A level 3 reply is allowed if the community allows links. |
| Private ask | "DM me" | Do not DM. Reply in public only. |
| Bump | "bump", "any update?" | Thread still open. Answer is wanted. |
| Author update | "thanks, will try X" | Problem may be solved. Lower value. |
| Author follow-up question | "does it work with Stripe?" | Strong reply target. |
| Wrong answer with votes | A top comment that misses the constraint | Chance to add a better answer. |

## Seller and fake asker signs

- Title looks like a question, body lists features.
- The account's other posts all link to one domain.
- Replies from new accounts agree with the poster in the same phrases.
- The post names a product in the first line.
- The "problem" matches the product page word for word.

Drop these threads. Do not reply to them.

## Timing habits

Active threads collect most of their comments in the first 6 to 24 hours. A reply in that window gets read. A reply after 7 days on a busy thread sits at the bottom. Quiet niche threads stay alive for weeks. Use the comment count and the age together: a 20-day-old thread with 4 comments is alive, a 20-day-old thread with 90 comments is closed.

## Triage list (use in this order)

1. Open and commentable.
2. The author describes a real situation with a detail.
3. The author asks a question or names a constraint.
4. Age under 30 days (Reddit) with fewer than 80 comments.
5. No comment already solves the exact constraint.
6. No seller signs.
7. The community allows helpful answers from members with a product connection, or allows any answer.

Spend a qualification only on threads that pass 1 to 6.
