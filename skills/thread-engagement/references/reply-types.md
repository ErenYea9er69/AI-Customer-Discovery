# Reply types

Each type has a shape. The shape sets the order of the sentences and the length.

## 1. Direct answer to a question (post level)

Shape: constraint, answer, one detail, optional trade-off.
Length: 40 to 110 words.
Example opening: "For a solo setup with under 10 clients, a spreadsheet with a due-date column and a weekly check does most of the work."

## 2. Tool request (comment level)

Shape: short answer to the question, one reason, an honest limit.
Length: 25 to 70 words.
Example opening: "I use a plain calendar rule for the first reminder and a template for the second."
At level 2 add one affiliation sentence and the product name once.

## 3. Script or template request

Shape: the words they asked for, in a plain sentence or two, then one tip on timing.
Length: 40 to 90 words.
Give the script as normal text, in quotes if needed. No bullets, no bold.

## 4. Switching request

Shape: ask or name the constraint, give two or three options with one trade-off each, put the owner's product among them only at level 2 and with affiliation.
Length: 60 to 120 words.
Never trash the competitor. Say what the competitor does well.

## 5. Complaint or rant

Shape: acknowledge in one short sentence, give one practical step, stop.
Length: 20 to 60 words.
Level 0 only. A rant does not ask for a product.

## 6. Add-on to a top comment

Shape: agree with what is right, add the missing piece, state the limit of the first advice.
Length: 30 to 80 words.

## 7. Answer to a direct question about you

Shape: yes or no, then the plain fact.
Length: 10 to 40 words.
Example: "Yes, I work on it, so weigh my take with that in mind. It does not handle payments, only reminders."

## Common rules

- First sentence answers the question.
- One idea per reply.
- No headers, no bullets, no bold, no emoji unless the voice samples use them.
- A number or a named tool beats a general statement.
- State one limit of your own advice. It builds trust and it is true.
- End when the idea ends. No sign-off.
